What are Gallbladder Stones?

Gallbladder stones, or gallstones, are solid particles that form in the gallbladder from bile components. These stones can vary in size and may cause severe pain, blockages, and infections in the bile ducts. One of the most common symptoms of gallstones is pain in the upper right abdomen. Gallbladder stones can be treated depending on their size, type, and the symptoms they cause. Factors such as diet, excess body weight, certain medical conditions, and some medications can increase the risk of developing gallstones. Regular medical check-ups and a healthy lifestyle can help manage and prevent gallstones.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main treatment for gallstones?

The primary treatment for symptomatic gallstones is a laparoscopic cholecystectomy, which is a minimally invasive surgery to remove the gallbladder. It is the gold standard because non-surgical remedies take years to work, rarely clear the stones completely, and carry a high rate of recurrence.

Is gallbladder removal safe?

Laparoscopic gallbladder removal is a commonly performed procedure. Your surgeon will assess your health and explain the risks, features-container, and recovery plan during consultation.

What happens if gallstones are not treated?

Untreated gallstones can cause recurring pain, infection, pancreatitis, jaundice, and increased surgical complexity.

Can you live without a gallbladder?

Yes. Most people live a normal, healthy life without a gallbladder after recovery.

What should I eat after gallbladder surgery?

Start with light meals, hydrate well, and follow your surgeon’s specific dietary advice while your body adjusts.

Can gallstones return after treatment?

The gallbladder is removed during definitive surgery, so gallstones cannot form there again.

What is laparoscopic cholecystectomy?

It is a minimally invasive procedure using small incisions and a camera to remove the gallbladder.
